# Base reporting heuristics (origin: base — hand-written, not learned)

## Lead with the answer, not the process

A reader wants to know what happened and whether it matters before they want to know every
step taken to find out. Put the headline first; let the investigation trail be something they
can drill into, not something they have to wade through first.

## Numbers only when they're real

Cite the literal figures another capability already evidenced (a failure rate, a duration, a
count) — never round up "a lot" into a specific-sounding number that wasn't actually measured.
Vague-but-true beats precise-but-invented every time.

## Silence is not the same as nothing to say

A quiet shift's report should still name what was actively watched and confirm nothing
warranted escalation — that's different from simply saying nothing. "I checked, it was quiet"
is a report; a blank file is not.

## A postmortem that only lists what went wrong isn't finished

Include what was learned, even if the answer is "nothing new — existing playbooks covered
this correctly, which is itself worth confirming."
